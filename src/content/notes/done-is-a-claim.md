On September 15, 2026, I had a lot of Cursor Ultra usage left, and the reset was coming. Leaving it on the table felt like a waste, so I decided to use as much of it as I could in one day.

![Cursor Spending page with usage limits resetting in 5 hours](/assets/notes/done-is-a-claim/cursor-spending-5-hours-before-reset.png)

![Cursor Spending page at the usage reset](/assets/notes/done-is-a-claim/cursor-spending-at-reset.png)

By my own count, I ran somewhere between 500 and 600 agents that day. I had agents planning work, agents doing research, agents doing the work, and agents checking on other agents. For a while it felt amazing. Every time I looked, more things had moved to Done.

![Cursor profile card showing agent and token stats](/assets/notes/done-is-a-claim/cursor-profile-card.png)

The agents weren't the problem, and neither was the quota. I was.

Every one of those agents eventually needed me. Someone had to read what it did, decide if it was right, and say yes or no. That someone was me, and there's only one of me. Somewhere in the afternoon I stopped really reviewing and started skimming. Then I started trusting the status column. Without noticing, "Done" went from meaning "finished" to meaning "an agent said it was finished."

What I took from it is simple. Done is a claim. It's an agent telling you something, and it still needs to be checked. A green checkmark from something that grades its own homework isn't proof.

It also changed how I think about my own role. Getting the agents to write code is the easy part now. What limits me is how much I can actually check. If I launch more work than I can verify, I'm not going faster. I'm just building a pile of claims I'll have to sort through later.

Running out the clock on my usage taught me more about my own limits than about the agents. They can go as wide as I let them. I can't, and that's fine, as long as I remember it.

Most importantly, as an AI Engineering student, this changed how I think about AI replacing humans. The agents replaced the typing. They didn't replace me — they moved me. My job became reviewing their work, answering their questions, and holding the whole thing in my head at once, and a human can only do so much of that. It took me 2 weeks to get most of that work reviewed and merged.

I do believe AI can make a real difference in the world. Not by replacing jobs, but by doing the work humans don't want or can't do: the easy, boring tasks, and the ones that would be impossible for one person to do alone at all.
